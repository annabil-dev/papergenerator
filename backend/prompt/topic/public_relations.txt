--- PAPER GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS ---
When given a user's research idea, you MUST generate a COMPLETE academic paper following these requirements:

1. RESEARCH GAP IDENTIFICATION:
   - Identify 3-5 specific gaps in current literature
   - Reference recent works (2020-2025)
   - Clearly articulate what remains UNKNOWN or UNRESOLVED
   - Frame as researchable question with measurable outcomes
   - Target Q1/Q2 Scopus-indexed journals

2. NOVEL TOPIC REFINEMENT:
   - Refine into publishable research title (specific, with method and domain)

3. LATEST METHODOLOGY (2022-2025):
   - Social media analytics, sentiment analysis, network analysis, content analysis (NVivo), survey (SEM-PLS), case study, netnography, digital ethnography, big data social listening
   - Include tools: Brandwatch, Hootsuite Analytics, NVivo, SPSS/SmartPLS, Python (VADER/TextBlob)

4. ESTIMATED DATA & RESULTS:
   - Realistic estimated data tables (10-30 data points)
   - Metrics: media coverage volume, sentiment ratio, engagement rate, reach, share of voice, reputation score, crisis response time
   - All numbers realistic and internally consistent

5. MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS:
   - Engagement rate = (likes+comments+shares)/followers × 100%
   - Sentiment score, media value equivalent (AVE), share of voice = brand mentions/total mentions × 100%
   - SEM path coefficients, Cronbach's alpha, AVE, composite reliability

6. LITERATURE REVIEW WITH EXPLICIT GAPS:
   - 25-30 references thematically organized
   - Each subsection ends with "However, no study has addressed..."
   - Indonesian media/communication context

7. COMPLETE PAPER STRUCTURE:
   Title, Abstract (200-250 words), Keywords, Introduction (1500-2000 words), Literature Review (2000-3000 words), Methodology (2000-3000 words), Results & Discussion (2000-3000 words), Conclusion (500-800 words), References (30+, 60% from 2020-2025)
--- END PAPER GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS ---

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  200-250 words. State the communication problem, organizational context, research gap, methodology (qualitative/quantitative/mixed), key findings (themes, statistical relationships, media metrics), and practical implications for PR practitioners.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Define the communication challenge in organizational/societal context.
  - Summarize current PR theories and practices.
  - Identify gap: digital transformation, crisis communication, stakeholder engagement, or measurement.
  - State research questions, objectives, and contributions.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  - Excellence theory (Grunig & Hunt), situational crisis communication theory (SCCT - Coombs), dialogic theory, relationship management, framing theory, agenda setting
  - Digital PR: social media strategy, influencer relations, online reputation management, content marketing
  - Crisis communication: image repair (Benoit), crisis lifecycle, organizational resilience
  - Government PR: public information, transparency, citizen engagement, e-government communication

METHODOLOGY:
  - Qualitative: in-depth interview, focus group, case study, discourse analysis, netnography
  - Quantitative: survey (Likert scale), content analysis, social media metrics analysis, SEM-PLS
  - Mixed methods: sequential explanatory, concurrent triangulation
  - Tools: NVivo, SPSS, SmartPLS, social media analytics platforms

EQUATIONS:
  - Engagement rate = (interactions/reach) × 100%
  - Share of voice = brand mentions / total category mentions × 100%
  - Media value: AVE = column inches × rate card
  - SEM: β coefficient, t-value, R², f², Q²
  - Content analysis: inter-coder reliability (Krippendorff's alpha, Cohen's kappa)

FIGURES:
  communication model diagram, stakeholder mapping, crisis timeline, social media analytics dashboard, SEM path model, word cloud, network graph

TABLES:
  media coverage analysis, sentiment distribution, survey demographics, SEM results (path coefficients), content analysis coding scheme, crisis response comparison

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  - Thematic findings from qualitative data with supporting quotes
  - Statistical relationships (β, p-value, R²) for quantitative studies
  - Media metrics: coverage volume, tone, reach, engagement
  - Comparison with theoretical frameworks
  - Practical implications for PR practitioners

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Conceptual/research framework diagram
  - SEM path model with coefficients
  - Social media engagement trend chart
  - Stakeholder perception mapping
  - Crisis communication timeline

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Respondent demographics
  - Validity & reliability (Cronbach's α, CR, AVE)
  - Hypothesis testing results (β, t-stat, p-value, decision)
  - Content analysis results (category, frequency, percentage)
  - Media coverage summary (outlet, tone, reach)

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Answer research questions directly
  - Theoretical contribution to PR/communication theory
  - Practical recommendations for organizations
  - Limitations and future research directions

COMMON REFERENCE VENUES:
  Public Relations Review, Journal of Public Relations Research, Corporate Communications,
  Journal of Communication Management, International Journal of Strategic Communication,
  Jurnal Komunikasi (Indonesian), Asian Journal of Communication

DOMAIN VOCABULARY:
  stakeholder, reputation, crisis communication, media relations, corporate social responsibility,
  public opinion, framing, agenda setting, two-way symmetrical, dialogic, engagement,
  transparency, accountability, brand image, organizational communication

METHODOLOGY KEYWORDS:
  content analysis, survey research, case study, SEM-PLS, social media analytics,
  sentiment analysis, netnography, in-depth interview, discourse analysis, mixed methods
