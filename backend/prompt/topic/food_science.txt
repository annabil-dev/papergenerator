   - Detail sample size calculations, validation strategy, and reproducibility measures

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  Structured or semi-structured abstracts should state the food material or system, research gap, objective, experimental design, analytical methods, primary quantitative outcomes, practical implication, and limitation in 150–250 words.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  Establish food safety, quality, nutrition, sustainability, or consumer relevance; define the product or process context; identify measurable gaps in current evidence; justify variables, controls, and analytical approach; end with clear research objectives or hypotheses.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Compare recent peer-reviewed studies on food composition, processing, microbiology, sensory response, shelf life, packaging, functional ingredients, sustainability metrics, and analytical methods; synthesize disagreements, methodological limits, and opportunities for replication or optimization.

METHODOLOGY:
  proximate analysis, sensory evaluation, shelf-life study, food processing, microbiology testing, chromatography, rheology, nutritional analysis, response surface methodology, life-cycle assessment, randomized experimental design, ANOVA, PCA, PLS regression, kinetic modelling, and validation against standards.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  Report composition changes, microbial counts, texture and rheological parameters, sensory scores, bioactive retention, shelf-life indicators, model performance, processing yield, environmental impacts, and trade-offs between safety, quality, cost, nutrition, and consumer acceptance.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  sensory spider chart, texture profile curve, chromatogram, microbial growth curve, process flow diagram, shelf-life plot, PCA biplot, response surface plot, kinetic degradation curve, packaging schematic, and consumer preference map.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  proximate composition, sensory scores, microbial counts, nutritional facts, processing conditions, shelf-life parameters, formulation matrix, ANOVA summary, model performance metrics, quality standards comparison, and cost or life-cycle inventory.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  Summarize the strongest evidence, quantify the main improvement or risk reduction, state the best-performing formulation or process condition, note practical constraints, and propose targeted validation, scale-up, or regulatory follow-up.
