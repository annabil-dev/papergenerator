================================================================================
TOPIC GUIDE — Theoretical Linguistics & Phonetics
================================================================================
FIELD: Arts and Humanities / Linguistics
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: APA
SUBDISCIPLINE: Fonetik, Morfologi, Sintaksis, Semantik, Pragmatik, Pemerolehan Bahasa, Sosiolinguistik

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–250 words. Reports: language(s) analyzed, linguistic level (phonology/syntax/semantics),
  theoretical framework, data type, key findings (e.g., "speakers of X language show Y pattern").
  Example: "Using optimality theory, this paper analyzes tonal variation in Javanese..."

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Identify the linguistic phenomenon under investigation.
  - Define key technical terms (phoneme, morpheme, constituent, deixis).
  - Situate within existing theoretical debate (e.g., Minimalist vs. Construction Grammar).
  - Research questions: "Does language X exhibit parameter Z predicted by Universal Grammar?"
  - Significance: theoretical implications for linguistic universals or typology.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Theoretical Background" / "Previous Research" / "Literature Review"
  - Review foundational theories: Chomsky's Generative Grammar, Optimality Theory (Prince & Smolensky),
    Construction Grammar (Goldberg), Role and Reference Grammar, Systemic Functional Linguistics (Halliday).
  - Cite key typological databases: WALS (World Atlas of Language Structures).
  - Review phonetic studies: Ladefoged & Maddieson, IPA conventions.
  - Discuss competing analyses of the same phenomenon.
  Key frameworks: Minimalist Program, OT, CxG, Exemplar Theory, Prototype Theory.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Data and Methodology" / "Method" / "Analysis Procedure"
  - Data collection: natural speech corpus, elicited production, judgment tasks, reading tasks.
  - Experimental phonetics: acoustic analysis (Praat), perception experiments.
  - Syntactic analysis: constituency tests, binding tests, movement diagnostics.
  - Corpus linguistics: corpus annotation (Brown Corpus, BNC, CHILDES).
  - Field linguistics: documentation of endangered languages.
  - Questionnaire/survey for sociolinguistic variation.
  Analysis tools: Praat (phonetics), R/SPSS (quantitative), ELAN (annotation).

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  Section name: "Analysis" / "Results" / "Discussion"
  - Phonetic: F0 (Hz), formant frequencies (F1/F2), VOT measurements, spectrograms.
  - Syntactic: tree diagrams, derivation proofs, paradigm tables.
  - Semantic: truth-conditional representation, prototype effects.
  - Sociolinguistic: social correlates of variation (age, gender, class), variation indices.
  Estimated data: "Acoustic analysis of 1,200 tokens from 24 speakers",
  "judgment tasks with 80 native speakers", "corpus of 2 million words".
  Tables: frequency of linguistic forms, phonetic measurement means ± SD.
  Figures: spectrograms, F1/F2 vowel plots, syntax trees, frequency charts.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Spectrogram of speech sounds.
  - F1/F2 vowel space plot.
  - Phrase structure tree diagram.
  - Semantic map across languages.
  - Social network diagram (sociolinguistics).

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Phoneme inventory (consonants × manner × place of articulation).
  - Morphological paradigm table.
  - Frequency of syntactic constructions.
  - Acoustic measurements (mean F0, VOT, formant values) by speaker group.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Summarizes linguistic findings and their theoretical implications.
  - Discusses support for or against specific theoretical model.
  - Limitations: sample size, language variety, elicitation method.
  - Future work: cross-linguistic comparison, experimental follow-up, corpus expansion.

ADDITIONAL SECTIONS:
  - "Language Background": description of the language(s) studied.
  - "Transcription Conventions": notation system used.
  - "Appendix": stimulus list, questionnaire, interlinear glossed examples.

COMMON REFERENCE VENUES:
  Language, Journal of Linguistics, Phonology, Lingua, Linguistic Inquiry,
  Natural Language & Linguistic Theory, Journal of Phonetics,
  Language Variation and Change, Applied Linguistics

DOMAIN VOCABULARY:
  phoneme, morpheme, syntax, semantics, pragmatics, prosody, allophone,
  constituent, head, complement, specifier, trace, movement, agree,
  deixis, presupposition, entailment, polysemy, prototype, register

METHODOLOGY KEYWORDS:
  corpus analysis, acoustic analysis, elicitation, judgment task, field linguistics,
  spectrographic analysis, optimality theory derivation, syntactic test, survey
