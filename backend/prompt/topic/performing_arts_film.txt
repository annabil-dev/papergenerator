================================================================================
TOPIC GUIDE — Performing Arts & Film Studies
================================================================================
FIELD: Arts and Humanities / Performing Arts / Cinematography
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: MLA or Chicago
SUBDISCIPLINE: Tari, Teater, Musikologi, Sinematografi, Studi Film

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–250 words. Reports: performance genre/medium analyzed, theoretical lens,
  period/cultural context, analytical findings, contribution to performance/film studies.
  Example metric: "analyzing 12 theatrical productions between 2010–2020..."

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Contextualize the performance form (theater, dance, film) within cultural tradition.
  - Define performance theory concepts (Brecht's Verfremdungseffekt, Stanislavski's method).
  - Identify research gap in existing performance scholarship.
  - State research question (qualitative): "How does choreography encode gender identity?"
  - Subsections: Performance Context, Theoretical Grounding, Research Scope.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Literature Review" / "Theoretical Framework"
  - Performance theory: Erving Goffman (performance of self), Richard Schechner (performance studies).
  - Film theory: auteur theory (Truffaut), psychoanalytic film theory (Metz), feminist film theory.
  - Dance theory: Rudolf Laban's movement analysis, Susan Foster on choreography.
  - Music analysis: Schenkerian analysis, ethnomusicology (Nettl), music and emotion.
  - Semiotics of cinema: Barthes, Christian Metz.
  Key frameworks: Gaze Theory, Narratology, Postcolonial Performance Studies.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Methodology" / "Research Design"
  - Performance analysis: systematic observation and notation of live/recorded performances.
  - Film analysis: shot-by-shot analysis, mise-en-scène analysis, sound design analysis.
  - Laban Movement Analysis (LMA) for dance research.
  - Ethnographic fieldwork: observation of rehearsal processes, backstage study.
  - Discourse analysis of scripts, screenplays, librettos.
  - Interviews with directors, performers, choreographers.
  - Archival research: historical performance documents, film archives.
  Data: video recordings, performance texts, ethnographic notes.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  Section name: "Analysis" / "Findings and Interpretation"
  - Description of specific scenes, movements, musical passages analyzed.
  - Interpretation of ideological, cultural, gender, political meanings.
  - Comparison across performance traditions or film genres.
  - Discussion of reception: audience responses, critical reviews.
  - Figures: film stills, choreographic notation, score excerpts.
  Tables: scene analysis matrix, character archetype comparison.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Film stills with mise-en-scène annotations.
  - Laban movement notation diagrams.
  - Timeline of scene structure (acts/sequences).
  - Music score excerpt.
  - Production design sketches (for theater research).

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Scene analysis: scene | technique | function | meaning.
  - Character comparison across productions.
  - Chronological evolution of film technique.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Synthesizes analytical findings into broader cultural/artistic significance.
  - Discusses implications for performance practice and scholarship.
  - Limitations: access to live performances, subjective interpretation.
  - Future work: expanded corpus, audience reception studies, cross-cultural comparison.

ADDITIONAL SECTIONS:
  - "Production Context": historical/cultural circumstances of creation.
  - "Reception History": how the work was received across different periods.

COMMON REFERENCE VENUES:
  Theatre Journal, Performance Research, Screen (film journal),
  Cinema Journal, Dance Research Journal, Journal of the Society for American Music,
  Music Analysis, TDR (The Drama Review)

DOMAIN VOCABULARY:
  mise-en-scène, diegesis, montage, choreography, libretto, score, dramaturgy,
  staging, blocking, cinematography, auteur, genre, narrative structure,
  performance studies, embodiment, gestus, theatricality

METHODOLOGY KEYWORDS:
  performance analysis, film analysis, Laban analysis, ethnographic observation,
  mise-en-scène analysis, discourse analysis, archival research, close reading
