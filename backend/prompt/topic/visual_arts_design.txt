================================================================================
TOPIC GUIDE — Visual Arts & Design
================================================================================
FIELD: Arts and Humanities / Fine Arts / Design
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: MLA or Chicago
SUBDISCIPLINE: Seni Rupa, Desain Grafis, Desain Interior, Desain Busana

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  Abstracts in visual arts/design papers are typically 150–250 words.
  Key elements: artistic medium, design methodology, cultural/aesthetic context,
  conceptual framework, visual analysis outcome, contribution to design discourse.
  Rarely reports quantitative metrics; instead uses qualitative descriptors
  (e.g., "this study employs semiotic analysis to decode...").

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Opening: contextualize the artwork/design phenomenon within contemporary culture.
  - Define key aesthetic, stylistic, or cultural concepts (e.g., postmodernism, vernacular design).
  - State the research gap: unexplored cultural symbolism, under-documented design movement.
  - Research questions are qualitative: "How does X visual element communicate Y meaning?"
  - Common subsections: Background, Artistic Context, Research Objectives.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Theoretical Framework" / "Review of Literature" / "Conceptual Background"
  - Review seminal art/design theory: Bauhaus principles, Gestalt theory, semiotics (Barthes, Saussure).
  - Discuss relevant art movements: Modernism, Postmodernism, Contemporary Art, Decolonial Art.
  - Review previous analyses of similar artworks/design objects.
  - Cite art historians (Ernst Gombrich, Rosalind Krauss) and design theorists (Victor Papanek).
  - Include visual culture theory (John Berger "Ways of Seeing", Susan Sontag).
  Frequently cited frameworks: Semiotic analysis, Iconology (Panofsky), Phenomenology.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Research Method" / "Methodology" / "Artistic Research Approach"
  Qualitative methods predominate:
  - Semiotic/visual analysis: decoding signs, symbols, and meanings in visual works.
  - Iconographic analysis (Panofsky's three levels): pre-iconographic, iconographic, iconological.
  - Formal analysis: composition, color, line, texture, space analysis.
  - Comparative analysis: comparing artworks across time periods or cultures.
  - Studio-based research: creative practice as research methodology.
  - Interview/ethnographic: interviewing artists, designers, curators.
  - Content analysis of design artifacts.
  Data: artwork images, design portfolio, museum collections, archival documents.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  Section name: "Analysis and Interpretation" / "Discussion" / "Findings"
  - Qualitative descriptions of visual elements (color palette, composition, symbolic motifs).
  - Interpretation of cultural/social messages embedded in artwork.
  - Comparison of design solutions across cultures.
  - Assessment of aesthetic value, innovation, functionality (for design).
  - Estimated discussion: 3–5 artworks/designs analyzed in detail.
  - Tables: comparative analysis matrix (artwork × element × meaning).
  - Figures: reproduction of artworks, annotated design diagrams, color palettes.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Reproductions of artworks/design samples with analysis annotations.
  - Timeline of artistic movement development.
  - Comparative visual matrix (side-by-side artwork comparison).
  - Design process flowchart (for design research).
  - Infographic of semiotic structure.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Comparative analysis: artwork | period | medium | symbolic meaning | cultural context.
  - Design element matrix: element | principle | application | reference.
  - Iconographic inventory of recurring motifs.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Summarizes visual/design interpretations and their cultural significance.
  - Reflects on contribution to art/design theory or practice.
  - Limitations: subjective interpretation, limited sample of artworks.
  - Future research: expanded corpus, cross-cultural comparison, digital art forms.
  - May include "Implications for Practice" (for design papers).

ADDITIONAL SECTIONS (field-specific):
  - "Artistic Context": situates work within broader art world/market.
  - "Cultural Significance": social/political meaning of the artwork.
  - "Technical Analysis": materials, techniques, conservation aspects.

COMMON REFERENCE VENUES:
  Design Studies, Journal of Design Research, Art Bulletin,
  International Journal of Art & Design Education, Leonardo (MIT Press),
  Design Issues, Visible Language

DOMAIN VOCABULARY:
  aesthetics, semiotics, iconography, visual culture, composition, typography,
  color theory, Gestalt, affordance, vernacular design, postmodern, bricolage,
  materiality, spatial design, wayfinding, branding, UX/UI, parametric design

METHODOLOGY KEYWORDS:
  semiotic analysis, formal analysis, iconographic analysis, visual discourse analysis,
  studio-based research, content analysis, phenomenological inquiry, case study
