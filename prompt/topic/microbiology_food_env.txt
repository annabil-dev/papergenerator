================================================================================
TOPIC GUIDE — Applied Microbiology (Food & Environmental)
================================================================================
FIELD: Agricultural and Biological Sciences / Microbiology
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: APA
SUBDISCIPLINE: Mikrobiologi Pangan, Mikrobiologi Lingkungan, Fermentasi, Probiotik

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–250 words. Reports: microorganism (genus, species), substrate/environment,
  methods (plate count, PCR, 16S rRNA), key findings (bacterial counts CFU/mL or CFU/g,
  inhibition zones mm, fermentation efficiency %).

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Food safety context: contamination statistics, foodborne illness burden.
  - Role of specific microorganism: pathogen, probiotic, spoilage organism, fermenter.
  - Research gap: unstudied food product, regional foodborne pathogen profile.
  - Objectives: isolation, identification, characterization, application.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Literature Review"
  - Taxonomy and characteristics of the target microorganism.
  - Virulence factors (for pathogens) or probiotic criteria (for beneficial microbes).
  - Detection and enumeration methods (standard plate count, MPN, PCR, ELISA).
  - Food safety regulations: SNI, Codex Alimentarius, FDA limits.
  - Fermentation microbiology: LAB in fermented foods, starter cultures.
  - Environmental microbiology: indicator organisms (E. coli, coliform), water quality.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Materials and Methods"
  - Sample collection: food/environmental samples (n=30–100), aseptic technique.
  - Isolation: selective/differential media (EMB, MSA, TCBS, de Man-Rogosa-Sharpe).
  - Enumeration: pour plate/spread plate method; total plate count, coliform count.
  - Identification: morphological (Gram stain, colony morphology), biochemical (API strips),
    molecular (16S rRNA PCR and sequencing).
  - Antimicrobial susceptibility: disk diffusion (Kirby-Bauer); MIC/MBC determination.
  - Fermentation study: pH, titrable acidity, bacterial count over time (0–72h).
  - Statistical: descriptive statistics, one-way ANOVA.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  - Total plate count: raw milk 10^4–10^6 CFU/mL; processed food 10^2–10^4.
  - Coliform count: should be <10 CFU/g (food safety limit).
  - Inhibition zone: probiotic vs. pathogen 8–20 mm.
  - Fermentation: pH drops from 6.5 to 3.8–4.5 over 24h; acidity increases 0.2 → 0.8%.
  Tables: isolation source, identification results, antimicrobial profile.
  Figures: bacterial growth curve, inhibition zone photo, phylogenetic tree (16S rRNA).

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Growth curve over fermentation time.
  - Inhibition zone photograph.
  - Phylogenetic tree (16S rRNA).
  - Colony morphology photograph.
  - Bar chart: bacterial count by sample type.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Isolate characteristics (colony morphology, Gram stain, biochemical tests).
  - Antimicrobial susceptibility (antibiotic | inhibition zone mm | category S/I/R).
  - Total microbial load by sample source with regulatory limit comparison.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Isolates identified and their significance stated.
  - Food safety implications: compliance/non-compliance with standards.
  - Probiotic potential assessment against criteria (acid/bile tolerance, safety).
  - Future work: genome sequencing, industrial fermentation optimization.

COMMON REFERENCE VENUES:
  Food Microbiology, International Journal of Food Microbiology,
  Applied and Environmental Microbiology, Journal of Food Protection,
  Letters in Applied Microbiology, LWT - Food Science and Technology

DOMAIN VOCABULARY:
  CFU, coliform, E. coli, LAB, Lactobacillus, Streptococcus, Staphylococcus,
  selective media, MIC, MBC, disk diffusion, 16S rRNA, API strip, Gram stain,
  biofilm, quorum sensing, sporulation, fermentation, titratable acidity

METHODOLOGY KEYWORDS:
  plate counting, MPN method, disk diffusion, API strip, 16S rRNA sequencing,
  RAPD-PCR, MALDI-TOF, ELISA, serial dilution, pour plate, spread plate
