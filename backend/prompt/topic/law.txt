ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150-250 words. State the phenomenon, population, setting, theory, data, method, key result, implication, and citation conventions. Use Legal citation (Bluebook/OSCOLA) with APA or Chicago for socio-legal studies consistently and report ethical safeguards where human participants, vulnerable groups, legal materials, or sensitive records are involved.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Establish the field context, urgency, and social or institutional relevance.
  - Define the core problem and affected population, doctrine, service setting, or behavior.
  - Identify the empirical, theoretical, methodological, or policy gap.
  - State research questions, hypotheses, doctrinal questions, or evaluation objectives.
  - Clarify contribution to theory, practice, policy, rights, equity, or institutional design.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Literature Review" / "Theoretical Framework" / "Legal Framework"
  - Foundational theories, doctrines, practice frameworks, and contemporary debates.
  - Prior empirical findings, doctrinal authorities, policy reports, and systematic reviews.
  - Measurement, validity, interpretation, causality, ethics, and context-specific debates.
  - Comparative, regional, historical, digital, and interdisciplinary scholarship.
  - Clear synthesis leading to the research gap and analytical framework.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Methods" / "Methodology" / "Legal Method"
  - Designs: qualitative, quantitative, mixed-method, doctrinal, comparative, evaluative, or review-based.
  - Data: participants, cases, statutes, judgments, surveys, interviews, archives, administrative records, or digital traces.
  - Instruments: validated scales, interview guides, codebooks, legal matrices, observation rubrics, or extraction forms.
  - Analysis: thematic coding, regression, SEM, case comparison, statutory interpretation, content analysis, or program evaluation.
  - Quality: reliability, validity, reflexivity, triangulation, citation accuracy, consent, confidentiality, and reproducibility.

EQUATIONS AND FORMULAS:
  Content analysis inter-rater reliability (Cohen kappa), proportionality tests, regression for empirical legal studies, chi-square for case outcome distributions

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Conceptual or theoretical framework diagram
  - Trend, comparison, or effect-size visualization
  - Process, pathway, institutional, or logic-model diagram
  - Thematic, network, or comparative case map

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Sample, case, document, or participant profile
  - Coding framework, doctrine matrix, or theme table
  - Statistical, evaluation, or comparative results table
  - Limitation, validity, risk, ethics, or reform matrix

RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  - Present patterns, mechanisms, interpretations, model estimates, doctrinal tests, or program outcomes.
  - Compare findings with theory, prior studies, legal authority, policy benchmarks, or practice standards.
  - Discuss equity, rights, ethics, cultural context, institutional feasibility, and unintended consequences.
  - Separate descriptive evidence, causal claims, normative argument, and reform recommendations.
  Tables: demographics, case matrix, model estimates, theme matrix, policy comparison, outcome indicators.
  Figures: conceptual framework, process flow, trend chart, network diagram, logic model, doctrinal map.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Answer the research question directly and field-specifically.
  - State theoretical, empirical, doctrinal, policy, or practice contribution.
  - Note limits in sampling, jurisdiction, measurement, causality, positionality, or source availability.
  - Recommend practice, policy, legal, institutional, or research actions.
  - Propose longitudinal, comparative, participatory, replication, or implementation research.

METHODOLOGY KEYWORDS:
  doctrinal analysis, case-law review, statutory interpretation, comparative law, empirical legal studies, judgment coding, proportionality analysis, socio-legal interviews
