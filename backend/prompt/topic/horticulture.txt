================================================================================
TOPIC GUIDE — Horticulture
================================================================================
FIELD: Agricultural and Biological Sciences / Agriculture
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: APA
SUBDISCIPLINE: Hortikultura Buah, Hortikultura Sayuran, Florikultura, Pascapanen

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–250 words. Reports: horticultural crop species (with Latin name), treatment type
  (pruning, growth regulator, storage condition), design, key results (yield/fruit quality parameters,
  shelf life days, vitamin C content mg/100g).

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Importance of the crop (nutritional value, economic value, market demand).
  - Production constraints (postharvest losses, low quality, short shelf life).
  - Research gap: effect of specific treatment not yet studied in this variety/region.
  - Objectives (3): "To determine the effect of...", "To find the optimal dose of..."

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Literature Review"
  - Review crop physiology: fruit set, ripening hormones (ethylene, auxin, gibberellin).
  - Postharvest technology: respiration rate, ethylene production, MAP (modified atmosphere packaging).
  - Growth regulators: IAA, IBA, GA3, 6-BAP, ethephon modes of action.
  - Previous studies on similar crops: tomato shelf life, mango quality.
  - Nutritional composition studies: vitamin C (ascorbic acid), carotenoids, anthocyanins.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Materials and Methods"
  - Experimental design: Completely Randomized Design (CRD) or RCBD.
  - Greenhouse/controlled environment or field study.
  - Treatments: concentrations of growth regulator/fertilizer/storage treatment.
  - Variables measured: fruit weight (g), fruit diameter (cm), sugar content (Brix°), acidity (pH/titratable acidity), vitamin C (mg/100g), chlorophyll content, shelf life (days).
  - Statistical analysis: ANOVA, DMRT 5%.
  - Equipment: refractometer (Brix), spectrophotometer (vitamin C), texture analyzer.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  - Fruit weight: tomato 80–200 g; mango 200–500 g; strawberry 15–30 g.
  - Brix° (sugar content): tomato 4–8; mango 14–20; strawberry 7–12.
  - Vitamin C: papaya 60–80 mg/100g; guava 200–300 mg/100g.
  - Shelf life: with treatment 14–21 days; control 7–10 days.
  - Significant at P<0.05 with DMRT grouping.
  Tables: mean comparison with DMRT, nutritional analysis table.
  Figures: fruit quality bar charts, growth curve, postharvest quality trend.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Bar charts: fruit weight, Brix°, vitamin C by treatment.
  - Line graph: quality changes during storage.
  - Photo: fruit at harvest/post-treatment.
  - Growth curve: plant height or shoot length over time.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Physical quality: fruit weight | diameter | firmness by treatment ± SD.
  - Chemical quality: Brix° | pH | vitamin C | anthocyanin by treatment.
  - ANOVA summary table.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - States optimal treatment and its practical implication.
  - Economic relevance: reduced postharvest losses.
  - Limitations: single cultivar, seasonal variation.
  - Recommendation for further study: multi-season, commercial scale.

COMMON REFERENCE VENUES:
  Scientia Horticulturae, HortScience, Postharvest Biology and Technology,
  Journal of Horticultural Science & Biotechnology, Acta Horticulturae,
  Journal of Applied Horticulture

DOMAIN VOCABULARY:
  cultivar, Brix, titratable acidity, ethylene, auxin, gibberellin, cytokinin,
  MAP, respiration rate, ripening, carotenoid, anthocyanin, chlorophyll,
  vitamin C, polyphenol, antioxidant, shelf life, postharvest

METHODOLOGY KEYWORDS:
  CRD, RCBD, ANOVA, DMRT, spectrophotometry, refractometry, texture analysis,
  gas chromatography (ethylene), controlled atmosphere storage, field trial
